WHAT THIS IS
------------

The part of Portuguese invoicing that is law rather than design. Four things:

  DocumentSeries    a registered run of gapless numbers for one document type and year
  Atcud             the document's unique code: the series' AT validation code, a hyphen, the number
  DocumentSignature the RSA signature, and the four characters of it printed on the page
  QrCodePayload     the string inside the QR square

Plus the Invoice aggregate that ties them together, and SignatureSource, which builds the exact
string the signature is computed over.


I DID NOT WRITE THESE FROM MEMORY
---------------------------------

Every prescribed detail here was checked against a primary source before it was implemented, and
then tested against the worked examples those sources publish. Two things I had wrong from memory
and would have shipped:

  - The ATCUD in the 2020 QR specification's example has no hyphen (CSDF7T5H0035). The current AT
    guidance is validationCode-number with a hyphen, and that is what this produces. The old
    example is stale, not an alternative format.

  - I had the four printed characters at positions 1, 11, 21, 31 but had not confirmed the
    indexing. Portaria 363/2010 article 6 states "a 1.a, 11.a, 21.a e 31.a posicoes", which is
    1-based. Off by one would have put four plausible characters on every document that verify
    against nothing.

The two signature examples from the DGCI specification are in the test suite character for
character:

    2008-03-10;2008-03-10T15:58:00;FT 1/1;28.07;
    2008-09-16;2008-09-16T15:58:00;FT 1/2;235.15;F8952fjEClltx2tF9m6/...

And the QR field list is checked against the published worked example, field by field.


THE RULES WORTH KNOWING
-----------------------

A series cannot issue before the AT has validated it. The validation code is half of every ATCUD
the series produces, so Activate() refuses without one rather than letting somebody find out at
the first audit. It is recorded once and never changed, because it is already baked into every
document the series has issued.

A series code is letters and digits only. It goes straight into "FT SERIE2026/35", and a space or
a slash there breaks the field that separates the series from the number.

Numbers come out one at a time. There is no reserve, no batch, no peek — a number taken and not
used is a gap, and a gap is the thing the whole mechanism exists to prevent. TakeNextNumber
mutates the series, so a caller that fails afterwards must roll the whole transaction back. Pass 2
gives the repository a GetForIssuingAsync that takes a row lock, so two tills at once queue rather
than one of them failing.

A document is built in two moves: lines while it is a draft, then Issue() takes the number,
computes the totals, signs the result and freezes everything. That shape is forced by the law, not
chosen — the number cannot be taken until the document is complete, and the signature covers the
total, so the total cannot move afterwards.

Voiding keeps everything. The number, the figures, the signature, the place in the chain. Only
the status changes and a reason is added. A missing number is worse than a cancelled one.

The QR code is fixed at issue and never regenerated — not even when the document is voided. It is
a record of what was printed and handed over; the status an inspector acts on comes from the
SAF-T export, where it is a field in its own right.

An exempt line cannot exist without its legal basis. A rated category at zero percent is refused
too, because it is somebody meaning "exempt" and reaching for the wrong constructor — it would
pass every arithmetic check and land in the wrong pair of QR fields, where nothing would notice.

An invoice identifies its customer; a simplified invoice need not. A sale to somebody unidentified
is a different document type, not an invoice with a blank field.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
----------------

This is not certified software, and nothing here makes it certified. Issuing real invoices in
Portugal needs:

  - a software certification number from the AT, which is what field R of the QR code carries.
    It is "0" until then, and 0 is a legal value for software in development and a very illegal
    one for software issuing real documents;
  - a private key registered with the AT under that number;
  - each series declared to the AT, which returns the validation code this module needs;
  - the SAF-T export and the monthly communication of documents.

The first three are paperwork. The fourth is pass 3.

KNOWN LIMITS
------------

1. One tax region per document. The QR's J and K blocks exist for a document mixing mainland and
   island rates on one page, which needs establishments in both. Nothing here produces them.

2. Withholding tax (field P) and stamp duty (field M) are not modelled. Neither applies to parts
   distribution, and inventing fields nothing fills would be inventing requirements.

3. The series year must match the document date's year. That is a convention rather than a rule
   of the law — a series may legally span years — but it is the only shape an accountant can
   reconcile against a year's VAT returns without counting backwards.
